## The Scene

Bournemouth in the early 1990s was predominantly a handbag house town. The dominant sound in its clubs was commercial, pop-influenced house — the kind that filled floors without asking much of the audience. The serious progressive house sound existed at the edges: when big-name DJs came through, and in the sets of a small number of local DJs who were playing something different.

This is the environment Simon Latham and Pete Lunn went out in most weekends — learning what they wanted to make by listening to what was and wasn't there.

## Handbag vs. The New Sound

Handbag house — sometimes disparagingly named for its association with a more mainstream, chart-adjacent crowd — was the default setting for most Bournemouth venues. It was accessible, functional, and not what Soundsation would go on to make.

The progressive house sound arrived in Bournemouth mainly when the circuit brought it: big DJs visiting from London and the Midlands, playing the music that had been developing in Cream and Renaissance and the Haçienda. For the rest of the time, the local scene was what it was.

## The Local Exceptions

Two Bournemouth DJs played the new sound consistently:

- **John Langford** — a local DJ operating at the progressive end of the spectrum
- **Gaz White** — another local figure who played outside the handbag mainstream

These were the DJs who gave Simon and Pete access to the progressive sound on regular nights, rather than waiting for the big names to come through.

## Artist Story — Dave Seaman at the Opera House

**Evidence status: Firsthand recollection by Simon Latham.**

Simon Latham was in the Opera House, around 1997, when Dave Seaman played *Do You Feel It?* — the Stress Records release Seaman's own label had put out. Simon and Pete watched from the balcony first, seeing the crowd respond to the record from above, before going down to the dancefloor.

The image is precise: the balcony view, the crowd below, your own record playing, the decision to go down. Seaman had brought the progressive house circuit to the town where Soundsation had formed. The record that connected them was being played in the room where Simon had spent years going out as a clubber.

## Artist Story — A Reported Sasha Play at the Opera House

**Evidence status: Report relayed to Simon by a friend; not independently confirmed for this venue.**

In 1996, a friend told Simon that Sasha had played *Do You Feel It?* at the Opera House in Bournemouth. Simon initially assumed his friend had mistaken the track.

Sasha’s play of the record at Cream, Liverpool on 15 June 1996 is independently documented. That confirmed support makes the Bournemouth report plausible, but it does not verify the specific Opera House appearance. The archive therefore retains this only as a reported artist story.

## Why This Matters to the Soundsation Story

The Bournemouth context explains something specific about how Soundsation's sound formed. Simon and Pete were not in a city where the progressive house sound was the default — they were in a seaside town where it was the exception. They had to seek it out: in the sets of local outliers like Langford and White, and in the visits of the bigger names playing the circuit.

Knowing what you want to make partly means knowing what's missing from what you're hearing. Bournemouth's handbag mainstream, and the progressive sound that cut through it when the right DJ or the right night arrived — that contrast is part of the education.
